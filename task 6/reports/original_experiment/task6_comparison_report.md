## 2. Engineered ratio features

| Feature | Formula | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| gross_to_fixed_gross | gross_pay / monthly_fixed_gross | Actual gross vs the employee's fixed entitlement. Zero pay (0) and wrong-decimal pay (~10x) sit far from the normal 0.3-3 range. |
| deduction_ratio | total_deductions / gross_pay | Share of gross taken in deductions. Excess tax pushes it above 1; a missing deduction lowers it. NaN when gross_pay is 0. |
| net_pay_ratio | net_pay / gross_pay | Take-home share of gross. Negative or tiny values signal over-deduction. NaN when gross_pay is 0. |
| gross_vs_expected | gross_pay / (monthly_fixed_gross * payable_days / days_in_month + overtime_pay + variable_pay) | Checks the data-dictionary identity 'gross = earned fixed + overtime + variable'. Should be ~1.0; a shifted decimal breaks it. NaN when the expected gross is 0. |
| fixed_gross_vs_role_median | monthly_fixed_gross / median monthly_fixed_gross of the same role_id | Pay relative to the role's norm. A salary far above its role is a role/grade mismatch. NaN for a role with no reference. |
| fixed_gross_change_vs_prev | monthly_fixed_gross / the employee's previous-month monthly_fixed_gross | Month-on-month change in the fixed salary. A drop suggests payment on a stale (pre-revision) salary; a large jump suggests a wrong grade. NaN for an employee's first month. |
| overtime_hours_paid | overtime_hours_paid (raw) | Overtime hours are capped at 50 by policy; very large values indicate an overtime spike. |
| overtime_pay_share | overtime_pay / gross_pay | Overtime as a share of gross. Normal is near 0; a spike dominates the payslip. NaN when gross_pay is 0. |
| pf_vs_prev_month | pf_employee / the employee's previous-month pf_employee (only when the previous PF > 0) | PF is stable month to month; a drop to 0 means provident fund was not deducted. NaN when there is no previous month or previous PF was 0 (PF-exempt employees). |

Undefined ratios (division by zero, missing history) are **NaN, not 0**. History features use previous months only.

## 3. Business rules

| Rule name | Condition | Business reason | Potential false positives | Output flag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rule_duplicate_payment | Same employee_id + month AND identical gross_pay, total_deductions and net_pay as an earlier row (file order). Only 2nd+ copies flagged. | A person should be paid once per month. | Genuine off-cycle or corrected payslips with the same amounts; file order may not equal processing order. | Potential duplicate - needs confirmation against payment/bank references (none in this dataset). |
| rule_negative_net | net_pay < 0 | Take-home pay cannot be negative. | Recovery of a prior overpayment could legitimately net negative. | rule_negative_net |
| rule_zero_net | net_pay == 0 AND payable_days > 0 | An employee with payable days should receive pay; fully unpaid months have payable_days = 0. | Unrecorded unpaid leave or full-and-final settlement months. | rule_zero_net |
| rule_gross_mismatch | /gross_pay - expected_gross/ / expected_gross > 0.001  (expected = fixed*payable_days/days_in_month + overtime + variable) | Data-dictionary identity for gross pay; breaks when an amount is mis-keyed (e.g. a shifted decimal). | Rows where gross includes components not in this dataset (arrears, adjustments). | rule_gross_mismatch |
| rule_overtime_cap | overtime_hours_paid > 50 OR (overtime_hours_paid > 0 AND overtime_eligible != 'Y') | Policy caps overtime at 50 hours and only eligible roles earn it. | Approved exceptions to the cap. | rule_overtime_cap |
| rule_missed_pf | pf_employee == 0 AND gross_pay > 0 AND the employee's previous-month pf_employee > 0 | PF is a statutory monthly deduction; a stop without history of exemption is suspicious. | PF opt-out after a wage change; first month cannot be checked (no history). | rule_missed_pf |
| rule_salary_drop | monthly_fixed_gross < 0.99 x previous-month monthly_fixed_gross | Salaries are revised upward; a drop suggests payment on the pre-revision salary. | Real salary cuts/demotions; the month AFTER an erroneous spike also looks like a drop. | rule_salary_drop |
| rule_fixed_gross_jump | monthly_fixed_gross > 1.5 x previous-month monthly_fixed_gross | A >50% one-month rise needs a promotion record; none exists in this dataset. | Promotions with large increments. | rule_fixed_gross_jump |
| rule_role_pay_outlier | monthly_fixed_gross > 2.0 x median monthly_fixed_gross of the same role_id | Pay should be close to the role's norm; double is a role/grade mismatch candidate. | Genuine outliers (rare skills, retention packages); roles with very few holders. | rule_role_pay_outlier |
| rule_invalid_amounts | Any required money / day column is NaN or non-finite | A payslip with a missing or invalid amount cannot be trusted. | Intentionally blank optional fields (none among required columns). | rule_invalid_amounts |

Thresholds come from the data dictionary / policy or label-free reference data and are **not tuned on the labels** (overtime cap 50 h, gross tolerance 0.001, role ratio 2.0, jump 1.5, drop 0.99). The *set of rules* was designed knowing the eight error-type names from the assignment, so rules-only results are optimistic compared with real payroll where error types are unknown in advance.

## 4. Isolation Forest configuration

- `scikit-learn` IsolationForest, `n_estimators=200`, `random_state=42`, `contamination=0.025`.
- Contamination 2.5% was fixed **before** seeing labels, to match the published `anomaly_baselines` payroll recipe so that features, not alert budget, explain differences. The labelled error rate is 1.36%; that number was not used.
- Features used (9): `gross_to_fixed_gross`, `deduction_ratio`, `net_pay_ratio`, `gross_vs_expected`, `fixed_gross_vs_role_median`, `fixed_gross_change_vs_prev`, `overtime_hours_paid`, `overtime_pay_share`, `pf_vs_prev_month`. Constant features dropped: none.
- Missing ratios are median-imputed using the training median (documented neutral choice; rules cover those cases).
- `if_flag = 1` means "statistically unusual", not "proven error". `if_anomaly_score`: higher = more abnormal. A fixed `score_threshold` can replace the contamination cut-off.
- Training is unsupervised and transductive (fitted on the payroll rows it scores). **No labels are used anywhere in fitting, preprocessing or feature selection.**

## 5. Three detection approaches

1. **Rules only** - flagged if at least one rule fires.
2. **Isolation Forest only** - flagged if `if_flag = 1`; no rule information.
3. **Combined** - `combined_flag = rules_only_flag OR if_flag` (exactly; verified by `tests/test_detector.py`). Individual rule flags and the model flag are kept in every output.

## 6. Evaluation methodology

- Per error type T: actual cases = payslips labelled T. **TP** = flagged and labelled T; **FN** = labelled T, not flagged; **FP** = flagged payslips with **no** error label (false alarms).
- Flags that land on a payslip carrying a *different* error type are neither TP nor FP for T (counted in `flagged_on_other_error_types`). `precision_strict` instead counts them as errors.
- Because the detectors do not predict *which* error type, every type shares the same false-alarm count, so per-type precision is dominated by it. Rule-specific precision is in section 9 (`task6_rule_hits.csv`).
- Precision = TP/(TP+FP), Recall = TP/(TP+FN), F1 = 2PR/(P+R). Undefined values are **n/a**, never 0.
- `ALL_ERRORS` pools the types; its precision/recall use the same definitions as the `anomaly_baselines` sheet.

**Why a transparent rule can catch what a statistical detector misses.** Isolation Forest only sees *unusual combinations of ratios*. A payslip that is an exact repeat of an ordinary payslip, or whose gross is 12% low, has ordinary-looking ratios, so nothing isolates it - but a rule that encodes a business fact (one payment per employee per month; gross = earned fixed + overtime + variable; net must not be negative) fires regardless of how common the amounts are. Measured here: the model caught 0 of 15 double payments and 1 of 12 stale-salary payslips.

Why each type behaves as it does:

- **DOUBLE_PAYMENT** (rules recall 1.000, model recall 0.000): an exact repeat of a normal payslip - every ratio is ordinary, so only a same-employee/same-month check can see it; a person sees the same payslip twice at once.
- **MISSED_DEDUCTION** (rules recall 0.933, model recall 0.200): PF is 0; invisible without knowing the employee normally pays PF (first-month cases have no history).
- **NEGATIVE_NET** (rules recall 1.000, model recall 1.000): net pay below zero - an explicit rule and an extreme net_pay_ratio.
- **OVERTIME_SPIKE** (rules recall 0.533, model recall 0.867): overtime is inflated, but only some spikes exceed the 50-hour policy cap; below the cap they resemble ordinary overtime.
- **ROLE_MISMATCH** (rules recall 1.000, model recall 1.000): fixed pay roughly 3x the role norm and a large jump over the previous month.
- **STALE_SALARY** (rules recall 1.000, model recall 0.083): gross and net are 12% too low while fixed gross is unchanged; a statistical outlier detector sees an ordinary-looking ratio, but the identity check does not.
- **WRONG_DECIMAL** (rules recall 1.000, model recall 1.000): gross/net are ~10x too high, breaking the 'gross = fixed + overtime + variable' identity and the gross-to-fixed ratio.
- **ZERO_PAY** (rules recall 1.000, model recall 0.200): gross and net exactly 0 despite payable days - an explicit rule; only 5 cases, so recall figures rest on very few records.

## 10. Honest findings that were not planned

- **`rule_salary_drop` produced 13 flags and 13 of them are false alarms (labelled: 0).** It was written to find stale salaries, but stale-salary payslips leave `monthly_fixed_gross` unchanged (median month-on-month ratio 1.00); instead their `gross_vs_expected` is 0.88, which the gross reconciliation rule catches. 13 of the 13 salary-drop flags are the month directly after a ROLE_MISMATCH payslip (the salary returning to normal). The rule is kept as specified because removing it after seeing labels would be tuning on the evaluation data; the ablation in `task6_sensitivity.csv` shows its effect and a payroll owner should decide whether to retire it.
- **Isolation Forest false alarms:** 128 unlabelled payslips; 55% of them carry variable pay (bonus/incentive) versus 16% of all payslips - legitimate bonus months look unusual to a ratio-only model.
- **Duplicate rule depends on file order.** It flags the later copy of an identical employee+month payslip. The sensitivity table shows the effect of reversing the input order and of flagging all copies. A payment/bank reference (absent from this dataset) is needed to decide which copy is the extra payment in production.

## 11. Which error type is hardest to catch? Why would a person find it easy?

**Hardest for the Isolation Forest alone (lowest recall, 0.000):** DOUBLE_PAYMENT (0/15).
- DOUBLE_PAYMENT: an exact repeat of a normal payslip - every ratio is ordinary, so only a same-employee/same-month check can see it; a person sees the same payslip twice at once.
Other types the model also largely misses (recall below 0.25): MISSED_DEDUCTION (3/15), STALE_SALARY (1/12), ZERO_PAY (1/5).
A person can apply a business fact that the ratios cannot express (see the notes above): notice the same payslip twice, or that a payslip does not add up. Rarity is not the cause: the rarest type (ZERO_PAY, 5 cases) is not among the hardest for the model.

**Hardest for the combined detector (lowest recall, 0.867):** OVERTIME_SPIKE (13/15).
The missed overtime payslips paid 6, 12 hours; ordinary payslips with overtime have a median of 8.5 h and a maximum of 29 h, and those employees' other months reach up to 26, 10 h. So these two are **not obviously wrong from this data alone**; a person would also struggle unless they could see the approved timesheet - context that is missing from the dataset.
The missed PF case has no previous month for that employee (`prev_pf_employee` is empty), so 'PF was dropped' cannot be established; a person with the employee's contract/PF enrolment record could tell.
**Limitations of this answer:** sample sizes are small (5-15 cases per type, so one missed payslip moves recall by 7-20 points), there are no confidence intervals, and the labels are synthetic injections.

## 13. Limitations and recommended next steps

- Labels are injected, synthetic errors with crisp signatures; real payroll errors are messier. Passing unit tests and good numbers here do not prove real-world performance.
- The rule set was designed with knowledge of the eight error types; thresholds were not tuned on labels, but rules-only results are still optimistic.
- No `unlock_CX-06` sheet exists in the workbook; `payroll_anomaly_labels` was used. Confirm that is what the assignment intends.
- Role medians are fitted on all rows (batch reference). In production, fit them on a historical window.
- The baseline recipe is not fully specified, so the replica only approximates the published figures (section 8).
- Next steps: add payment/bank references for duplicates; add a per-employee overtime history feature; have a payroll owner review thresholds and retire or rework `rule_salary_drop`; validate on a later, unseen period; consider a priority score to reduce the OR-combination's false-alarm load.
